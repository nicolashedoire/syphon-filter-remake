# French PS1 actor-data survey and candidate initializer

Research-only, 2026-11-06. No gameplay changes. The French executable `SCES_019.11` has SHA-256 `059c6cbfabe686555b4bb45c61c3116b42eadbf1ac9c94b10ae4416891c38a95`; the French disc BIN has SHA-256 `c0f54d8110eb87b802cb6cadb304251fa5910eafa193a5de16cd2c2651911e72`. Tools: REA 6.8.0 / Node 24.22.2 and Rizin 0.9.1; Ghidra 12.1.4 / ARM64 MIPS little-endian `MIPS:LE:32:default`, with only a manually defined candidate function and no global analysis. Native community parser: `references/sf-pc-port/src/assets/mission_objects.cpp`, an independently developed US-port interpretation, not verified original French source.

## All 20 resource tables

Read the locally available French disc, located each `/FOG/<name>.FOG`, selected `<name>.BIN` and ran the community parser on all 20. All parse successfully, yielding **4,556 total object records**, of which **617 have decoded class ID 1**, and **559 of those have a decoded nonempty path**. These are counts of *authored records*, not enemy counts, spawns, simultaneous occupants, proof of hostility, or behavioral traces. Zero class-1 records does not mean no enemies: `PARK2.BIN` has zero class-1 records but separately contains a class-60 record referencing `HANS.TMD`.

For every table, an independent small raw-byte check re-read the header, 76-byte object rows and 20-byte definitions, independently counted class-1 IDs and model-name strings, and matched the C++ parser. This cross-check does not validate health semantics, route semantics, enemy identity, or activation order. Individual table digests, class-1 counts, route counts and model names are in [the per-level inventory](ps1-all-level-actor-summary.csv). Applying the candidate selector calculation described below to the initial `+0x24` words yields indices 0–25 for all 617 class-1 records; index 8 occurs 265 times. This cross-check assumes, without proof, that the routine applies to these particular records and uses their unchanged initial values; it does not establish weapon identity or runtime behavior. Full parsed records are retained in the local investigation; fields and path decoding inherit the community parser's interpretation. US resource/catalog associations are candidates until connected to French runtime behavior.

Reproduction with the French CUE/BIN, a C++20 compiler and the community parser:

```sh
python3 tools/audit_ps1_actor_tables.py --output work/new-actor-audit.json
```

The script verifies the disc SHA-256 and refuses to overwrite an existing output. BIN hashes cover the FOG entry's entire declared sector extent including potential padding.

## REA/Rizin and Ghidra cross-check

A French routine candidate at `0x8005895c` has the two-word prologue `0x27bdffc8`, `0xafb1002c` that the community US port uses as a guard for an NPC initialization routine at US address `0x8005805c`. Two instructions alone are not a unique signature: four such sequences exist in the French executable. Its stronger structural similarity (object index at `param+2`, stride `0x4c`, record field `+0x24`, table stride `0x20`) motivates the hypothesis but does not prove function identity or which actors call it.

- REA/Rizin at `0x80058a44–0x80058a6c` reads a signed halfword at `param+2`, calculates `index * 76 + [0x80116540]`, then reads byte `+0x24` from that record.
- At `0x80058a74–0x80058ab4`, a nonzero value is shifted left five; a zero value instead uses bits `0x3000` of the word at `+0x24` to select 19, 20 or 0. One byte at `0x8010ca9d + index*32` is read and stored to the second structure's `+0x38`. `0x8010ca9d` is one byte past the base of the 26-record table previously identified as candidate weapon data. The field's actual meaning and whether it controls shooting are unproved.
- Ghidra's manually defined function independently shows that table lookup and further control flow: it reuses the selector to read a word at `0x8010caa0 + index*32`, shifts right three, masks `7`, and passes that three-bit result to `0x80023a40`. Its role is unknown. Ghidra's inferred types and variable names are not original symbols and its uninitialized global-pointer variable requires caution.
- The candidate also writes a 100-valued halfword to a pointed-to structure at `+0x34` (`0x80058a00–0x80058a08`). Nothing observed proves this field is health or a difficulty value.

Evidence retained locally (not included in this repository): `npc-init-candidate.json`, `npc-init-tail.json`, `npc-init-selector.json`; REA records completion and unchanged executable hash. `ghidra-npc.log` contains the disassembly/decompiler results generated by `tools/ghidra/ExportNpcCandidate.java` from the header-stripped copy `work/ghidra-alt/psx-payload.bin` at base `0x80010000`. No cross-version instruction-by-instruction equivalence and no runtime breakpoint were established. The identification of the function as NPC initializer therefore remains a hypothesis.
